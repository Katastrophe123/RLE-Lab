Week 2 · Day 7 · Notes

Publish the judgment, not just the code

Week 2's story is about trust: measuring a judge, finding its holes, and showing how much of a gain was real. That's what evaluation and post-training teams look for.

1

The write-up, in six parts

SectionKey numbers (always with n)
Task and rubricRubric items and weights
Judge report (Day 2)Agreement and kappa on the holdout; human ceiling; false positives
Training run (Day 3)Training vs held-out judge per checkpoint; chosen checkpoint and why
Attacks and defenses (Day 4)Attack success before → after; gold agreement before → after
Comparison (Day 6)Win rate [CI], sign test, length-controlled win rate, average lengths, spot check
LimitationsOne labeler, small holdout, 0.5B policy, 3B judge, one dataset, one seed
2

Release safely

  • Licenses: read the license of every dataset you used. Often you can share your own labels and your model's outputs with a link to the original dataset, without re-uploading its text. When unsure, don't redistribute. (Not legal advice.)
  • Personal data: scan for emails, phone numbers and real names; remove or replace them. Never publish anything from work without permission.
  • Clean: validate every record against a schema, remove duplicates (a repeated attack inflates your catalog), and split by task so nobody tests on what they tuned on.
3

Cards: dataset and model

Dataset card: what's inside and how many records; how it was made; who labeled it; known problems (e.g. a single labeler); license; what it should not be used for.

Model card (for your adapter): add a "Training reward" section naming the judge, rubric version, reward formula, the judge's known biases, and the held-out judge used for evaluation.

4

Post and interviews

Post hook (example): "My RL-trained model won 60% of comparisons. After controlling for length: 53%." Then 3–4 plain sentences, one chart, the link.

Two new 60-second answers: "How do you know an LLM judge is trustworthy?" (gold set, human ceiling, kappa, false positives, calibration) and "How would you stop a model gaming a judge?" (attack catalog, delimiters, code checks, held-out judge, length control).

Glossary

Dataset card
A README for a dataset: contents, origin, labelers, license, limits.
Model card
A README for a model, including how it was trained and evaluated.
License
What others may do with your work.
Personal data (PII)
Information that identifies a real person, e.g. emails or phone numbers.
Schema
The fields each record must have, and their types.
Deduplication
Removing repeated records.
Group split
A train/test split that keeps every record of a group on one side.
Write-up
The README or report that explains what you did and found.
Null result
A finding of no clear difference. Still worth reporting.

Publish week 2

Four release helpers, then the write-up, the dataset card, a post and your week 3 pick.

Tasks

  1. Write tools/release_check.py with:
    • validate_record(rec, schema): schema maps field → type (or tuple of types); returns a sorted list of missing:f, type:f, extra:f; bool doesn't count as int
    • find_pii(text): {"emails": [...], "phones": [...]} in order; a phone has 10+ digits, allowing spaces, dashes, dots, brackets and a leading +
    • dedupe(records, key): keep the first per key (strings compared lower-cased with whitespace collapsed); returns (kept, n_removed)
    • split_by_group(records, group, test_groups): (train, test), keeping each group on one side
  2. Pass the 20 tests on the right, then run them on your real catalog and labels.
  3. Publish the week 2 write-up, DATASET_CARD.md, and the model card section.
  4. Draft your post, and send me your week 3 pick.
