Week 2 · Day 2 · Notes

Measure the judge before you trust it

During training the policy pushes on every weakness the judge has. Measuring first tells you how far to trust the reward.

1

The gold set and the human ceiling

  • 50–100 real answers from your own model, a mix of good and bad. Grade each with the same rubric the judge uses.
  • Blind: grade before you look at the judge's scores, or you'll drift toward them.
  • Human ceiling: have a second person grade 20 of them. If two careful people agree 75% of the time, a judge at 95% would be suspicious, not impressive.
  • Split it: about 38 items to tune the judge prompt on, about 12 as a holdout you only check at the end.
2

Agreement, and why kappa

Percent agreement = matching grades ÷ total. Simple, but a lazy judge that says "pass" to everything agrees 90% of the time when 90% of answers are good, and catches nothing.

Cohen's kappa removes the agreement you'd expect by luck:

kappa = (observed − chance) / (1 − chance)
chance = Σ over labels of  P(judge says label) × P(human says label)
Worked example (50 answers)Value
Both pass 30 · both fail 10 · judge-only pass 6 · human-only pass 4
Observed agreement40 / 50 = 0.80
Chance: judge passes 36/50 = 0.72, human passes 34/50 = 0.680.72·0.68 + 0.28·0.32 = 0.579
Kappa(0.80 − 0.579) / (1 − 0.579) ≈ 0.52

Kappa 0 = no better than luck, 1 = perfect, negative = worse than luck. A commonly used rough guide treats 0.4–0.6 as moderate and above 0.6 as substantial. It's a convention, not a law.

4

Scores: Spearman and calibration

  • Spearman correlation compares the order: sort the answers by your score and by the judge's score; 1 = same order, 0 = unrelated, −1 = reversed. Ties get the average of their ranks. GRPO only needs the order within a group to be right.
  • Calibration: group answers by judge score (1–4, 5–6, 7–8, 9–10) and compute your pass rate in each group. It should rise from group to group. If the 9s and the 6s pass at the same rate, the scores carry no information.
5

Read every miss, then iterate

For each disagreement write a short reason ("lenient on length", "missed an invented fact", "fooled by formatting"). Two or three causes usually explain most misses, and each points to a fix in the judge prompt or rubric. Re-measure on the tune set; check the holdout only at the end.

Glossary

Gold label
A grade given by a person, used as the answer key.
Gold set
50–100 real answers with blind human grades.
Human ceiling
How often two careful people agree. The best a judge can sensibly reach.
Percent agreement
Matching grades ÷ total.
Chance agreement
How often two raters would agree by luck, given how often each says each label.
Cohen's kappa
(observed − chance) / (1 − chance). Agreement with luck removed.
False positive
The judge passes an answer a person failed.
False negative
The judge fails an answer a person passed.
Spearman correlation
Correlation of ranks: are two lists in the same order?
Calibration
Whether higher scores really mean better answers more often.
Holdout
Gold items you never tune on, used only for the final numbers.
